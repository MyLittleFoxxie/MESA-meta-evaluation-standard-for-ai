The central problem is not generic opacity but evidence fit: a conclusion is only as strong as the alignment among the claim, the measurement oracle and its implementation, the evidence mode, and the provenance retained to audit that evidence. We study this through a controlled mock harness, an original two-provider repeated-run cohort of 16 synthetic prompts (640 responses), a prospective 36-prompt machine-checkable cohort (1439 usable responses across two provider/model strata), a source-backed Verifiability Evidence Matrix (VEM), and a provenance self-audit. The prospective cohort did not satisfy its pre-specified broad bidirectional mismatch gate after corrective analysis. A narrower directional result remained: lexical understatement of executable-test agreement occurred in 9 of 12 code prompts and 13 of 24 provider-by-prompt cells. The corrective analysis also exposed an adjudication-fidelity condition within measurement validity. The historical verifier returned PARTIAL; two specification-justified repairs, applied without new model calls, changed the same-evidence verdict to NOT_CONFIRMED. A 2 × 2 decomposition showed that abstention repair alone reduced overstatement-positive cells from 7 to 1, while admissibility repair reduced them to 2 under the frozen threshold. A second implementation derived from the same written contract matched all 1439 response-level adjudications; a post-hoc specification-derived validation suite passed 21/21 fixtures in both implementations and detected all 11 declared pure mutants. Only 1 of 9 mutants applied to the real cohort crossed the final decision boundary. Together with the VEM and provenance audit, these results support a practical rule: define the claim first, use a claim-matched oracle, qualify the verifier that implements that oracle, select evidence modes appropriate to the claim, and preserve claim-critical provenance.
