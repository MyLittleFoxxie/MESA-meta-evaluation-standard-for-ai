This multivocal literature review analyzed 352 artifacts from academic and industry sources, identifying 32 highly relevant studies through rigorous screening. We examine three dimensions across user-centered quality aspects: stakeholder contributions, evaluation methodologies, and metric maturity. The findings reveal significant gaps: while functional quality (70%) and trustworthiness (60%) have mature metrics, critical dimensions, such as practical utility (20%), accountability (18%), and sustainability (12%), remain under-instrumented. Current evaluation approaches, such as standards-based frameworks, benchmarks, automated tools, and human-in-the-loop methods, operate in silos, forcing users to navigate fragmented tools without domain-specific guidance. Multistakeholder consortiums dominate (28.1%), but regulatory representation remains limited (6.3%). No single approach provides a comprehensive coverage of user needs. The technical-user divide persists, as most frameworks require specialist expertise and focus on model performance over real-world utility. This study highlights the urgent need for unified and accessible evaluation frameworks that balance technical rigor with practical usability, enabling informed genAI adoption decisions across all stakeholder groups.
