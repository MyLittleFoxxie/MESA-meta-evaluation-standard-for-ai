This work introduces a reliability-aware perspective through an abstention-aware benchmarkfor Arabic emotion classification. The benchmark is based on 1002 manually annotated sentences across five emotion categories,with very high inter-annotator agreement (κ = 0.9722). Our framework evaluates multiple LLMs under a unified zero-shot settingand explicitly models reliability through coverage, unknown-rate, and strict versus covered evaluation protocols. We further include acontrolled few-shot extension and supervised Arabic baselines (AraBERT and MARBERT) for contextual comparison. Results showthat GPT-4o and OpenAI-o3 achieve the strongest overall zero-shot performance, while few-shot prompting improves performancefor most models, particularly in accuracy and macro-F1, though gains are not fully uniform across systems. Importantly, wedemonstrate that predictive performance alone is insufficient to fully characterise real-world model behaviour, as reliability metricsreveal meaningful differences in output validity and deployment readiness. These findings highlight the importance of reliabilityawareevaluation for Arabic NLP and provide a more realistic framework for assessing LLMs in deployment-oriented scenarios.
