Independent researcher Description This record contains a theoretical manuscript on the lifetime of AI benchmarks in recursive data environments. The paper develops an origin-free framework for analyzing how benchmark validity changes over time when benchmark items, paraphrases, solution traces, model outputs, and evaluation conventions re-enter the public knowledge stream and can later affect both models and evaluators. The central object is benchmark half-life : the first time a benchmark lineage falls below half of its initial validity under a declared operational evaluation specification. Benchmark validity is modeled as the product of discriminative power and a construct-validity component, allowing the theory to distinguish among loss of score separation, loss of target-resolved comparisons, and loss of order fidelity. The manuscript introduces state variables for semantic leakage, verified novelty inflow, verification fidelity, support diversity, evaluator coupling, construct drift, and lineage observability. It derives jump-aware lifetime bounds, identification and partial-identification results, anchor-based construct-validity bounds, portfolio design results under common-mode decay and coverage uncertainty, and safe sequential monitoring and control results under ambiguity and partial observability. The work is intended for researchers in AI evaluation, benchmarking, model metrology, robust decision-making, and measurement theory. It is relevant to topics such as benchmark contamination, benchmark saturation, dynamic benchmarks, synthetic data, recursive corpora, validity auditing, and governance of evaluation systems.
