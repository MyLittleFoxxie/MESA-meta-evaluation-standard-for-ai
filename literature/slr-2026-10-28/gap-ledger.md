# Candidate gaps and counterexamples

Assistant synthesis; author review pending. “Observed problem” refers to evidence in particular sources. “Candidate gap” is an inference requiring further investigation. No claim below establishes that a solution is absent from the field.

| ID | Problem and status | Supporting evidence | Existing responses and counterexamples | Limits and uncertainty | Candidate MESA implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | Applying measurement reasoning remains difficult. Observed in sampled audits and reviewer applications. | S01 validity audit; OECD disagreement on Coverage and Specificity; R097 disagreements about scope and evidence. | ECBD explicitly connects decisions to evidence; EFPA supplies guidance; the Scorecard requires reasons. | Different tasks, samples and definitions; no pooled difficulty estimate. | Study whether readers make warranted judgments with supplied evidence. Do not claim MESA is the first bridge from measurement to AI. |
| G2 | Accessibility of a concise general review procedure across expertise levels is not settled by this corpus. Candidate question. | EFPA assumes expertise; OECD uses AI students; ECBD identifies user studies as future work. | BenchScout has a human tool study; Evaluation Cards has audience specific modes and feedback; PRAISE has a teaching case with nonprogrammers. | Bounded search, unresolved texts and differing meanings of accessibility. Neither brevity nor self reported confidence is demonstrated understanding. | Define an audience and measure interpretation and effort before claiming improved accessibility. |
| G3 | Review outputs may obscure the difference between missing evidence and a negative result. Documented variation in answer rules. | BetterBench documentation rubrics; Scorecard nonfulfilment rule; SIGHT No/NR category. | EFPA already separates some forms of missing information; OECD permits N/A under uncertainty; Evaluation Cards displays gaps. | Some instruments intentionally assess reporting compliance. Their rules are not necessarily errors for that purpose. | Test interpretation of Yes, Partly, No, Not sure and Not applicable without assuming distinct options ensure correct use. |
| G4 | The relation between a shorter review and its retained coverage needs evidence. Candidate question. | Existing frameworks cover different concerns: S01, S03, S05, S07, B03, R097. | ECBD already has 20 questions, and other tools provide summaries or selected criteria. | Number of questions is not workload. More coverage can increase ambiguity or effort; fewer questions can hide separate judgments. | Trace retained and omitted functions; assess what cannot be concluded from MESA. Do not infer completeness from a completed form. |
| G5 | Reproducible execution and supported score interpretation can diverge. Established conceptual distinction with audit examples. | B02 implementation issues; B11 prompt effects; R291 construct evidence; B20 contamination measures. | BetterBench, ECBD, Evaluation Cards and Croissant Tasks address parts of the relation. | A review form cannot independently verify code, training data or every causal explanation. | Help readers identify which evidence is present and where technical follow up is needed. Do not claim a questionnaire validates the benchmark. |
| G6 | Broad capability claims can exceed a benchmark’s task coverage. Observed and conceptual concern. | S01, S05 Capability/Specificity; S06 and B08 alternative AGI frameworks; B09 safety/capability correlations. | These sources already define frameworks and propose validation approaches. | No consensus on every capability construct; cognitive categories are not interchangeable with benchmark tasks. | Prompt appropriate scope of interpretation. Do not make general intelligence assessment MESA’s default purpose. |

## Claims this review does not support

- No previous benchmark review framework exists.
- Existing approaches never explain terminology or offer concise forms.
- No prior work considers readers, effort or accessibility.
- OECD’s facets are a quality ranking or can be averaged into one score.
- MESA is easier, faster or more accurate because it has 25 questions.
- Agreement between models validates a questionnaire or predicts human agreement.
- The retrieved literature proves that MESA fills a unique gap.
