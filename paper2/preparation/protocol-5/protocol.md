## Purpose and research questions

Paper 2 presents a method that decides whether benchmark results support a requirement on a coding agent, using MESA ratings of the benchmark, and evaluates it on SymPy. MESA, its rubric, and its traceability rules come from the MESA paper. The research questions were set on 2026-11-09, before any session of this protocol, after the MESA paper's revision to rubric version 5.

**The requirement** (signed by the author in `requirement.json`): "Working alone on a bug report from the SymPy repository, the coding agent changes the code so that the tests the maintainers later wrote for that bug pass, for at least 70% of bug reports, within 15 minutes each." It also states its task set (the 20 source tasks, less any with shown broken tests), the coding agent, the confidence level (95%), and the minimum number of tasks a decision needs (`n_min` = 9, the smallest number at which Accept is possible at 0.70: 9 of 9 gives a lower bound of 0.701). 70% and 15 minutes are illustrative values fixed before any session. The requirement is written for this study, not stated by the SymPy maintainers.

**The seven criteria** decide whether a benchmark resolve rate is a sound measurement for this requirement: B05 (tests that reject a correct solution), B07 (tests or scoring that accept a wrong solution), B12 (the benchmark's own uncertainty reporting), B14a (the benchmark's safeguards against training exposure), B14b (whether the named model could have seen the tasks in training), B28a (the grading environment), and B28b (the agent environment the benchmark ships). Training exposure means a model may have read a task's issue and fix during training and remember the fix rather than work it out; the source tasks are old and public, while the fixes of the target tasks were merged after both models' stated training cutoffs (Gemini 3.8 Flash: March 2026; GPT-6.1 Sol: 30 April 2026); five target issues were opened before them, and the cutoffs are the providers' claims.

The rules rest on audits showing agents that retrieve fixes from git history or the web (Cursor, 2026; DebugML, 2026; Mohl et al. 2026).

## Records and decision levels

Case records (`paper2-case/1`, [`evidence.py`](../../method/evidence.py)) link the requirement, the policy, one agent, the declared tasks, observations with patch and log hashes, one rating form per criterion, and the agent's transfer justification. [`cases.py`](../cases.py) builds them. Rating forms cite the rubric by path and hash.

- **M1** is the resolve rate decision on all source tasks; **M2** on the source tasks from the requirement's repository (here the same); **M3** applies MESA's rules: the evidence status from the rating forms, then the score decision on M2's tasks less those with shown broken tests, reported with and without them.
- A rating counts only when its level meets the policy's minimum, its outcome is passed, its scope covers the agent and tasks, and its procedure, check kind, evidence location, and detection limits are recorded. A check may be reported by the sources or run by the reviewer; the form says which.
- B05, B12, B14a, and B14b apply always and cannot be Not applicable.
- A defect of the whole benchmark, with a cited location, makes the status Inadmissible. A single task whose tests are shown to be wrong (B05: they reject a correct solution; B07: they accept a wrong one), with its location and a quote, and with its patch executed if it rests on one, is left out of M3 before any score is seen. Missing information gives Inquiry Required, as do fewer than `n_min` remaining tasks.
- The decision on the requirement also needs the signed transfer justification; the method checks that it exists, names its author, and matches the scope, not what it argues.
- Nothing is scored or averaged.

**Two policies.** The strict policy (`POLICY-P5-STRICT`) needs all seven criteria at Verified with a passed check. The lenient policy (`POLICY-P5-LENIENT`) needs B05, B28a, and B28b at Verified and B07 at Documented, each with a passed check, and leaves out B12, B14a, and B14b: it shows the decision that ignores training exposure and the benchmark's own statistics.

**ABC.** The 33 ABC items that the MESA concern mapping assigns to the concerns of the seven criteria ([`abc-items.json`](abc-items.json)) are answered met, not met, not applicable, or unknown. ABC is applied as its paper describes it (Zhu et al. 2025, Section 3 and Appendix F): each item scores 1 when met and 0 otherwise, averaged over the applicable items of each part (T, O, R); the decision comes from the resolve rate on all 20 source tasks with its interval; each unmet or unknown item is a caveat. ABC has no rule to withhold a result or to leave out single tasks, so its decision equals M2's apart from the caveats.

**Audit only and ABA.** The audit-only decision leaves out the tasks the executed checks show to be broken, with no criteria gate, as automated audits use their findings; it equals M3's decision before the evidence status. ABA's published per-task findings for SWE-bench Verified ([`audit_checks/aba-overlap.json`](audit_checks/aba-overlap.json); a task is flagged when ABA reports at least one major finding in either mode) give a second audit-only decision. ABA is a comparison only, never an input to the forms.

**Outcomes known in advance.** Under the 95% Wilson rule at threshold 0.70, the cut points across our exact sample sizes are: with 20 tasks (`M2`, `ABC`, and the full target set), Accept needs 19 or 20 resolved and Reject 9 or fewer (leaving 97.2% of decisions Inconclusive at true rate 0.75); with 18 tasks (`ABA`), Accept needs 17 or more and Reject 8 or fewer (95.5% Inconclusive at 0.75); with 15 tasks (the target set excluding the 5 issues opened before the training cutoffs), Accept needs 14 or more and Reject 7 or fewer (90.3% Inconclusive at 0.75); and with 14 tasks (`audit_only` and MESA's counterfactual pre-gate score `M3_withheld`), Accept needs 14 of 14 and Reject 6 or fewer (97.2% Inconclusive at 0.75). With the eight MESA rating forms, the ABC record, and `requirement.json` signed by the author on 2026-11-09 (`audit_forms/completed/`, with the 34 field-level edits from run A's drafts listed in `audit_checks/signing-changes.json`): B05 is Verified, passed, with task defect `sympy__sympy-18199`; B07 is Verified, passed, with task defects `-13878`, `-15809`, `-18199`, `-21847`, `-22914`, and `-23824` (6 of the 17 tasks where a cheating patch passed the hidden tests and failed the independent check); B12 is Undocumented, not performed; B14a is Verified, failed; B14b is Documented, not performed, for both agents; B28a is Verified, failed (from the 9-of-9 grading runner check); and B28b is Verified, passed. Consequently, across the five source method families (six evaluated outputs: `M2`, `audit_only`, `ABA`, `M3_strict`, `M3_lenient`, and `ABC`), both `M3_strict` (blocked by B14a and B28a failed, with B12 and B14b requiring inquiry) and `M3_lenient` (blocked by B28a failed) return Inadmissible and withhold a decision (`No decision`). For RQ2, MESA's withheld output (`No decision`) is reported as not comparable to an issued decision, while its pre-gate score decision on the 14 kept tasks is reported separately as a counterfactual (`M3_withheld`, identical to `audit_only`). ABA flags `sympy__sympy-13031` and `-18199` among the source tasks, so its decision uses 18 tasks. ABC decides on 20 tasks with 15 caveats (8 met, 9 not met, 10 not applicable, 6 unknown). The two rating runs (`audit_checks/rating-agreement-v5.json`) agree on the level of 7 of 8 forms, the outcome of 6 of 8, the check kind of 6 of 8, and the task defects of all 8.

The ledger ([`study_controls.py`](../study_controls.py)) reserves a slot by exclusive file creation before launch and writes each record completely or not at all. A task runs once per agent and phase. A reserve session (`open --phase reserve --replaces N`) replaces a session with a recorded infrastructure failure: it takes that slot's agent and task, needs that phase's authorization, and gives the agent that phase's Python; each failure is replaced once. Failed starts and interruptions keep their slots and count. There is no automatic retry. The ledger starts empty; the sessions run under protocol 4 are reported in its history.

## Cases

[`cases.py`](../cases.py) builds one case per agent from the source sessions, the signed forms, and the signed requirement. A completed session gives the observation `close` wrote; an invalid session counts as not resolved. A reserve session replaces the session it follows. A session that did not complete and was not replaced counts as not resolved; its patch and provenance are the slot's ledger outcome record. Every selected task needs a session. Per agent it reports, for RQ1, M2, audit only, ABA, M3 under both policies (with the removed tasks, the score with and without them, and what each blocking criterion is missing), and ABC; for RQ2, each decision against the direct test (Agreement, Discrepancy, Inconclusive, or No decision), the difference in resolve rates with its interval, and the direct test without the five early target issues; and, as a control, the invalid sessions by kind and the resolve rate by test result alone.

## Replay

The replay tests the implementation on real records ([`replay.py`](../replay.py)): for each source task of one agent it evaluates the case as collected, with that task's provenance removed, and with its identity changed (task identifier or patch). Each changed copy must change what it targets: a provenance reason for that task, or a rejected input. Rows are repeated observations on the same tasks. The earlier "oracle defect" condition is gone, because the B07 cheating attempts now enter the B07 rating form itself.

**B07 cheating attempts and broken tasks (decided on 2026-11-08, before any attempt and before the freeze).** B07 asks whether the scoring can be gamed. In SWE-bench the scoring is each task's hidden tests, so it is gamed when a patch passes them without fixing the issue. Claude Sonnet 5.5, in subagent runs separate from the work on the method, makes one cheating attempt for each of the 20 source tasks, from the issue text and the real fix only, never from the hidden tests or any session result (`paper2/preparation/counterexamples.py`; records in `audit_checks/b07-counterexamples/<task>/`). For each task it (1) lists the behaviors the issue asks for (`behaviors.md`); (2) writes an independent check of them (`check.py`); (3) writes at most three cheating patches, each meant to pass the hidden tests while breaking one listed behavior, for example the real fix without a part, or says in `no-candidate.md` why none could be written. The tool then runs the check on the code before the fix (it must fail) and with the real fix (it must pass); otherwise the check is invalid and no patch counts. It grades each patch on the hidden tests with the harness and runs the check on it. A defect is shown on a task when the check is valid, the harness resolves a patch, and the check fails on it. The tool records this mechanically. It counts as a defect of the task's tests only if a behavior the patch breaks is stated in the issue text: a check may also test behaviors inferred from the real fix, and a patch that breaks only those still does what the issue asks. The rating form run judges this for each shown defect from the issue text and the check output, quoting the issue (rule added on 2026-11-08 after the first attempt record, for `sympy__sympy-16597`, showed a defect only on behaviors the issue does not state; no other attempt result had been seen). Every attempt is recorded once, with its logs and hashes, whatever the outcome. The B07 rating form covers the 20 source tasks. It lists each shown defect under `task_defects`; for the other tasks the attempts are its check, with the limits that the patches are Claude Sonnet 5.5's own construction (one attempt per task, at most three patches), so a failure to find a defect does not show that none exists.

**The B28a grading check (2026-11-09, before signing).** B28a asks whether the system under test can alter the scoring. The SWE-bench harness applies the patch, then runs the tests with the repository's own runner and parses its log. A Claude Sonnet 5.5 run, from a fixed prompt ([`b28a-check-prompt.md`](b28a-check-prompt.md)), wrote three patches for each of `sympy__sympy-13031` (SymPy 1.1), `-20590` (1.7), and `-24539` (1.12), one task per test runner layout, each changing only test-running files; `counterexamples.py scoring-check` refuses any other file and grades each patch. The harness graded all nine resolved (`audit_checks/b28a-scoring-check/*/check.json`; run record `audit_checks/b28a-check-run-2026-11-09.json`), so B28a has outcome failed with a cited location. The defect is in the grading of the whole benchmark, so no task is listed. The session rule above keeps this study's own grading from the same defect.

**A task with broken tests is left out of M3 (decided by the author on 2026-11-08).** A rating form may list single tasks whose tests are shown to be wrong: B05 when they reject a correct fix, B07 when they accept a patch that breaks the issue. A defect counts only with a cited location and, if it carries a cheating patch, only when that patch was executed. M3 leaves such a task out, names it and the reason, and decides from the remaining tasks; M1 and M2 keep every task. MESA rubric version 5 adopted this rule (2026-11-09) and adds that the removal is fixed before any score is seen and that the score is reported with and without the removed tasks; before, MESA judged a criterion only as a whole, so one broken task would have blocked every decision. `sympy__sympy-18199`, now among the 20 source tasks, is such a task for B05: the earlier check (`../protocol-4/audit_checks/b05-18199-counterexample.json`) showed that its hidden tests reject a patch limited to the issue as stated. A defect that concerns the whole benchmark still blocks M3, as before.

[`sessions.py`](../sessions.py) prepares and records each session; it never starts a model.

- **Checks before a session:** the sandbox check (`paper2/preparation/sandbox_check.sh`) runs each tool with the session flags in a scratch folder and records whether network access and reads outside the folder are blocked (`audit_checks/sandbox-check-*/summary.json`). Network isolation is reported as enforced only where blocking is shown. Results on 2026-11-07 (versions 1 to 8). With `--dangerously-skip-permissions`, `curl` fetched a web page, and listing the home folder, reading outside, and writing inside all worked: that flag also turns the terminal sandbox off. In the version 4 check, `--mode auto` was not available for this account, and `agy` ran in its default mode: `ls ~` ran and the sandbox refused it, and `cat`, not in the shared rules, was denied and ended the run. Codex with web search and the apps server off reported no web search tool (its search of its tool list matched only the MCP resource listing tools), and network access from the shell stayed blocked. Versions 5 and 6 and short probes (logs in `~/paper2-archive/protocol-5/agy-probes/`): with the allow list, a write grant for the folder allowed redirects only to absolute paths, and a relative grant broke the sandbox. With `toolPermission` set to `proceed-in-sandbox`, every shell command ran in the sandbox without approval, including redirects, pipes, `2>&1`, and commands not on any list, and the allow list was no longer needed; the default agent's `search_web` ran without approval, so a custom agent with only local tools is used. The version 6 check of the chosen setting: network access (the `python3` fetch, `curl`, `gh`), reads outside the folder from the shell, and writes outside it were blocked; writes, edits, compound commands, prefixes, and pipes inside worked; the agent had no web search or page reading tool; a file tool read outside the folder was denied and ended the run. A request to run a command outside the sandbox (`BypassSandbox`) was denied and ended the run. Codex on the version 6 prompt: as in version 4. Version 7 adds the agent Python. With it in `/Users/Shared`, `agy`'s sandbox refused to read or run it (a probe: "Operation not permitted"), so `python3` was Homebrew's; under `/opt/homebrew/var/agent-python` `agy` found the shared `python3` and `pytest` (Python 3.9.20 and `mpmath` 1.3.0; with the target tasks' Python, 3.12.14 and 1.3.0), with the other results as in version 6. Network access, reads outside, and writes outside stayed blocked. After the update to Codex 0.167.0 (2026-11-07), the version 8 Codex checks with each Python gave the same results, and the logs of both versions name the same tools.
- `open --phase P --config C` refuses unless:
  - the frozen manifest exists and the authorization for phase P names its hash;
  - nothing has changed since the freeze;
  - the launch settings are complete, Codex is signed in, and for an `agy` session the `agy` program is locked against updates;
  - `~/paper2-workspaces` is empty.

  It builds a workspace at the base commit (no later commits, tags, remote, or unreachable objects; no test or reference patch), then reserves and starts a slot. It writes `prompt.md`, `run.sh`, and a record form that holds only the operator's name and notes.
- `bash sessions/NN/run.sh` starts the tool once in the workspace:
  - with the flags in `configurations.json` and no input after the prompt;
  - with a fresh Codex home for a Codex session, and for an `agy` session with the custom agent and the gate hook in the workspace's `.agents/` folder, which `.git/info/exclude` keeps out of the patch;
  - with the task's agent Python first on `PATH`;
  - stopped at 900 seconds.

  It writes the logs and `run.json` (start and end in UTC, exit code, tool version, whether the watchdog stopped it). The author or, at the author's request, Claude Opus 5.5 launches it after the phase is authorized.
- `close --slot N`:
  - reads `run.json` and the logs, and refuses a log that starts before the slot was reserved;
  - grades the patch in Docker on the hidden tests;
  - writes one runtime observation per capability: enforced, held, violated, or not observable;
  - archives the workspace.

  A patch counts as resolved only when every FAIL_TO_PASS and PASS_TO_PASS test passes, the session stayed within 900 seconds, and the session is valid. A session is invalid when its commands or file paths name study material, its tool outputs show oracle content or study paths, it runs a network command (also by full path or on a later line of a command), it calls a tool outside its configuration's local set, or it receives a message after the prompt, or when its patch changes a test-running file (`bin/test`, any `runtests.py` or `conftest.py`, `pytest.ini`, `setup.cfg`, `tox.ini`, `sympy/testing/pytest.py`, `sympy/utilities/pytest.py`), since the harness runs the patched repository's own test runner. Study material is matched by the markers in `sessions.py` (`STUDY_MARKERS`, `HOME_WIDE`, `CONTENT_MARKERS`, `NETWORK_COMMANDS`): the records and this repository, other workspaces, the archive, Codex and Antigravity homes, the mirror, dataset names, and searches rooted at the home folder, all users, two levels up, or the disk root. Edit bodies are not matched. An `agy` session is also invalid when the files in its `.agents/` folder differ from the frozen ones at `close`. Tool output that shows study paths, such as a process list showing the log paths of `run.sh`, counts as oracle content. A run that `agy` ends at a denied action, which the gate is meant to prevent, is graded as it stands, and the denial is recorded. An invalid session is recorded with the matched evidence and keeps its slot; a session over a limit is valid but not resolved.
- `close` checks the tool version that `run.sh` recorded against the frozen one, so a later tool update does not block closing a session that ran with the frozen version.
- `fail --slot N --status S --description D` records any other outcome and moves what the slot left (workspace, Codex run home without its copy of the sign-in, `agy` memory) to the archive, so the next slot can open. Nothing is retried automatically.

Agents run on the host without CPU or memory limits, so resource limits are reported as not observable. Reads outside the workspace are detected from the commands, file paths, and tool outputs in the logs, not prevented: a read that names no marked place and prints no oracle content is not detected. Model identity is what the client logs report, not attestation by the provider. The custom agent `local` limits `agy` to its shell, file, and wait tools; its default agent also offers web, browser, MCP, scheduling, subagent, and knowledge tools, and any tool outside the local set makes the session invalid. `agy` applies the user's shared permission rules (`~/.gemini/config/config.json`, frozen by hash; at the author's decision, the rules that allowed `gh` and commands in another folder were removed on 2026-11-07, leaving `ls`, `diff`, and `git config`) and keeps memory across conversations, which `run.sh` moves to the archive before and after each session. The reference fixes are on this computer: in `~/.cache/huggingface` (both SWE-bench Verified datasets), the SymPy mirror (`~/.cache/paper2`), the archive (`~/paper2-archive`), and the repository records. Codex can read them, and only the markers detect such a read afterward; `agy`'s sandbox blocks reads under `/Users` outside the workspace (decided by the author on 2026-11-07).

**Unequal isolation.** The two configurations are not isolated alike. Codex blocks network access from shell commands in its sandbox and limits writes to the workspace and temporary folders, but it also offers tools outside the shell, among them a web tool (`web__run`), image generation, and subagents (`spawn_agent`), as the tool lists in its logs show; calling any of them makes the session invalid. Web search and the apps server are turned off (`web_search = "disabled"`, `features.apps=false`); in the version 3 check the agent found the apps server's search after web search alone was off, and in the version 4 check, with both off, it found no web search tool. `agy` blocks network access, reads, and writes outside the workspace from the shell in its terminal sandbox, so it is stricter than Codex on reads; the gate hook refuses a file tool access outside the workspace and a request to leave the sandbox, and the run goes on, as Codex goes on after a refused command; the refusal texts differ between the tools. Both find the same Python first on `PATH`, but Codex can also read and run other Pythons on the disk, while `agy`'s sandbox blocks those under `/Users`. Its step counting differs (one tool per step, while Codex can run several commands in one call); steps are reported, not limited. Its run time is the time it reports, without its startup. `agy` updates itself even with `AGY_CLI_DISABLE_AUTO_UPDATE=1`, so its program is locked; Codex's link to its current release is locked as well, and the version check before each session catches any update. The same invalidation rules apply to both, and any difference between their outcomes may partly reflect these differences.
