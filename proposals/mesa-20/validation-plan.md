# Human testing plan

Proposed future work; not performed. Freeze and archive the exact draft and source packets before testing. The aims are to find out whether people understand the questions, detect consequential problems and strengths, reach defensible conclusions, and use less total effort.

## 1. Check the content with people

Ask people with measurement expertise, benchmark engineering experience and intended-user experience to review the questions independently. For each question, ask whether it is necessary, clear, distinct and answerable from realistic evidence. Ask what has been omitted, especially scorer checks, bias, broad claims and live evaluations. Collect examples rather than relying only on votes.

Separate the question “Does this topic belong?” from “Can a reviewer answer it reliably?” Do not retain 20 at the expense of critical content. Set panel size and the rule for revising disputed items before recruitment; justify them from the study purpose and resources. This proposal does not invent a statistically adequate panel size.

## 2. Observe people using it

Run a formative think-aloud study with intended users of different experience levels. Ask them to explain selected questions in their own words and show how they locate evidence and choose answers. Record confusion, hidden subquestions, source searches, repeated work and requests to consult the full template. Revise the form and freeze a new version before comparative testing.

Record source-finding time, reading/judgment time, writing time and expansion time separately. Include guide reading and training. Record assistance, including AI use. Do not turn observations from this formative phase into a claim that the final form is validated.

## 3. Compare forms on unseen cases

Compare MESA-20 with the current 40-item scorecard and full template using the same intended uses and fixed source packets. Include new benchmarks beyond HLE, ARC-AGI-2 and DesignQA: a software-scored test, an AI-judged task, an interactive/agent environment, a multilingual or multimodal case, and a live or refreshed evaluation. Include both well-supported and problematic cases.

Randomize and counterbalance assignments so form, case difficulty and reviewer experience are not confounded. Avoid giving the same reviewer the same benchmark in multiple forms, which would let earlier work teach later answers. Each case/form needs multiple independent reviews. Plan the required number of cases and reviewers using the precision needed for the chosen outcomes, with statistical advice; do not select a convenient sample and call it adequate afterward.

Use a separate expert panel to identify consequential strengths, gaps and permissible uses from the full evidence packets before seeing checklist results. Keep disagreements in that reference assessment visible. It is a reasoned comparison standard, not error-free ground truth. Blind outcome assessors to form where practical.

## 4. Measure the outcomes that matter

| Outcome | Record and compare |
| --- | --- |
| Missed critical issues | Whether a review misses an issue that would change the use recommendation; review serious misses individually. |
| Unsupported criticism | Whether missing evidence is presented as proven failure, or a narrow test is rejected only for being narrow. |
| Preserved strengths | Whether supported useful aspects survive the compression. |
| Decision quality | Whether conclusions follow from evidence and state conditions and limits. |
| Reviewer agreement | Agreement on judgments and critical-use conclusions; separate applicability disagreement. Report raw agreement and a justified chance-adjusted measure with uncertainty. Do not treat the new categories as an ordered numeric scale. |
| Total effort | Full time distribution, including source discovery, training, writing and expansions; record abandoned or incomplete reviews. |
| Understandability | Misread terms, unsupported answers, help requests and perceived effort. |
| Expansion burden | Frequency, reasons and time spent opening the full template or splitting grouped questions. |

Predefine the acceptable loss in issue detection and the desired effort reduction before examining comparative results. Explain why those bounds suit the use. Do not choose a pass threshold from whichever result looks favorable. Account for repeated observations from the same reviewer and benchmark.

Internal consistency alone is not an appropriate success criterion: these questions intentionally cover different aspects of quality. Nor does high agreement establish correct judgments if everyone overlooks the same evidence.

## 5. Decide whether to adopt it

Adopt as a routine form only if important findings and defensible recommendations survive, burden falls after including expansions, and users understand Unknown versus Weakness. Revise if grouped questions hide critical differences. Use the full template when the detailed profile is needed. Keep the pilot and human-study evidence distinct in the thesis, and publish the tested version, methods, exclusions and negative results.
