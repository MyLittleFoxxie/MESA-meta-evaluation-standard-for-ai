# Proposal: MESA in 20 questions

Historical draft 0.1. The revised [MESA-25 form](../mesa-25/questionnaire.md) supersedes this form as of 2026-10-01.

Draft 0.1, started 2026-09-29 and completed 2026-09-30. Prepared with AI assistance at the author's request. This is a new proposal, not a replacement of the current MESA template, paper, scorecard or reviews.

**Recommendation: use 20 questions as a concise review framework, with a short guide and a separate research appendix. Do not claim that 20 questions preserve every EFPA or MESA check.** They can preserve the main reasoning about what a score supports, while making the report shorter and reducing repeated judgments. Equal coverage in use, faster completion and reviewer agreement remain untested.

Start with the [questionnaire](questionnaire.md). Use the [guide](guide.md) to answer it. The [coverage map](coverage-map.md), [source notes](sources.md), [retrospective checks](checks.md), and [human testing plan](validation-plan.md) explain and qualify the proposal. Those records are not additional forms for every reviewer.

## What the source comparison changed

Protogerou and Hagger's Q-SSP assesses survey **studies**, not just the quality of questionnaires. The attached file has 67 PDF pages: the checklist is on pp. 53-54 and its explanation starts on p. 55. A short checklist therefore does not eliminate the supporting knowledge. The paper develops its checklist through literature work, expert input and empirical checks; it also reports limited support for criterion validity (pp. 26-33). MESA can borrow the development approach, but cannot inherit its validation. [Source S1](sources.md).

Q-SSP's response options and optional overall scoring are not adopted. Its numeric coding combines No and Not stated clearly; MESA needs to preserve the difference between missing evidence and a demonstrated problem. This proposal also avoids a percentage pass mark: a missing critical scorer study cannot be compensated for by well-written documentation. [Sources S1-S3](sources.md).

EFPA explicitly permits combining rating categories while preserving the inadequate/adequate boundary and supplying a key. MESA-20 therefore uses Supported, Weakness, Unknown and Not applicable, mapped respectively to 2-4, 1, 0 and n/a. This loses distinctions among adequate, good and excellent. It retains the critical-gap rule and judgment-based conclusion. The scale choice is a design proposal, not a tested improvement. [Source S2](sources.md).

Buros contributes an approach to communicating findings: explain the instrument and its development, discuss technical evidence, then draw a practical conclusion. Its two supplied examples show that concise narrative can preserve both strengths and limits. They do not show that the underlying investigation is short. [Sources S6-S8](sources.md).

## What “20 questions” means here

| Component | Required record |
| --- | --- |
| Q1-Q4 | Four factual answers |
| Q5-Q19 | Fifteen evidence judgments, each with a source-linked note |
| Q20 | One reasoned recommendation |
| Outside the count | Reviewer, date, interests/conflicts, assistance used; a source list with versions, locations, access dates and restrictions |
| Conditional added work | Detailed analysis for a consequential scorer, score structure, subgroup, threshold, live test or reporting problem |

These are 20 top-level questions, not 20 yes/no facts or 20 independent quality dimensions. Q3, Q9, Q10, Q11, Q16 and Q17 bundle several related checks. If users must routinely expand all of them, the form has failed its workload goal even though its heading count is 20. The human study must count the actual work, including source gathering, note writing and expansions.

The blank questionnaire and guide together contain about 2,400 whitespace-separated words, compared with 21,488 in the full template and 14,222 in the scorecard. These counts exclude this proposal's research appendices and completed answers. They establish shorter reading material, not measured savings in research time; no PDF page-count claim is made.

The target is 20 questions **in total**, rather than 20 ratings plus the full Part A and three separate closing essays. This is a larger departure from the present scorecard, which keeps 40 rated items, 26 numbered descriptive units and closing sections. The current full template has 44 active numbered descriptive units and 87 rated items. These counts are directly checked, not taken from older planning estimates.

## What is preserved

- The exact benchmark and use under review, source traceability, and the distinction between facts and judgments.
- The chain from stated ability to tasks, score and claim, including narrow versus broad interpretations.
- Task quality, coverage, scorer checks, relevant references, stability, uncertainty, shortcuts, contamination, transfer and fair comparison.
- Practical inspection and reproduction, maintenance, honest reporting, and a final statement of supported and unsupported uses.
- Positive findings as well as limitations, with missing evidence clearly distinguished from evidence of failure.

## What is reduced or lost

Separate documentation-quality ratings are folded into the relevant evidence note. This saves repetition but makes it easier to confuse poor reporting with poor methods; the guide explicitly separates them. The seven section ratings and three subsidiary summary ratings disappear. Q20 carries their reasoning without a numerical profile.

Detailed classifications, report-development inventories and separate usability ratings disappear from routine completion. Reference types and sources of score variation share questions. Detailed item statistics, score-structure analysis and subgroup checks become conditional. These are substantive reductions in detail, not just shorter wording. The [item map](coverage-map.md) records the changes.

Human norming thresholds and human-test reliability formulas are not transferred automatically to AI. Where a benchmark makes a claim that depends on such a model, qualified technical review is still needed. A CHC-inspired ability map can help examine broad claims, but is not imposed on every benchmark or treated as a complete theory of AI ability.

## What the checks establish

The mapping accounts for all 131 current numbered MESA units. A retrospective reading found a destination for each of the 25 gap-register entries across the three reconciled pilots, and retained selected positive findings. The checks also expose statements in those reviews that should not be copied uncritically. See [checks](checks.md).

This is evidence of traceability and plausibility. It is **not** a fresh review of the benchmarks, an independent validation, or a demonstration that a new reviewer will notice the same issues. The pilot set helped shape the earlier scorecard and this draft, so it cannot provide an independent test of generalization.

## Decision for the project

Keep MESA-20 as a clearly versioned experimental form until the [human testing plan](validation-plan.md) is carried out. If it preserves important findings and reduces total effort on unseen cases, it could become the normal review form, with the existing template serving as detailed guidance. If its grouped questions conceal critical gaps, revise or increase the question count. Twenty is a design constraint from this request, not a scientifically established optimum.
