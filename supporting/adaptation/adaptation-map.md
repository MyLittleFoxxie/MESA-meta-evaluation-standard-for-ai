# Proposed EFPA–MESA correspondence

This is a conceptual explanation of the current questionnaire, not a recovered history of decisions, an endorsement, or an empirical result. Source: original EFPA Test Review Model 2025, all 90 PDF pages archived as text with page markers. Section numbers below refer to EFPA; Q numbers refer to MESA. The source distinguishes descriptive Part A from evaluative Part B. MESA's first five questions judge whether descriptive information is available; they do not reproduce Part A's factual record.

| MESA | Relevant EFPA function | Operation and reason | Remaining limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Part A §§1, 2, 3: identity, form, measurement | Combine version, task set and scoring identification | No complete provenance record |
| Q2 | Part A §2: content, population, use | Replace human population with intended AI systems; retain intended purpose | Stated purpose does not establish valid use |
| Q3 | Part A §2: response mode, stimuli, demands | Simplify tasks, inputs, answers and actions into a description check | Detailed interaction taxonomies omitted |
| Q4 | Part A §2: administration and conditions | Replace human administration with instructions, resources and limits | Does not enumerate every software/runtime setting |
| Q5 | Part A §§3–4: scoring and reports | Combine how responses become scores and how results are presented | Documentation is not evidence of scoring accuracy |
| Q6 | Part B §6 rationale; §10 validity | Retain explanation connecting tasks to claimed ability | A plausible explanation is weaker than empirical validation |
| Q7 | Part B §6 development; §10.1 content | Combine source and selection justification | Detailed item-selection analyses omitted |
| Q8 | Part B §6 item development and quality | Retain checking problems and reference answers for errors | No separate technical checklist for each audit method |
| Q9 | Part B §10.1 content evidence | Retain coverage relative to the claimed ability | Broad capability coverage requires further domain-specific judgment |
| Q10 | Part A §3 and Part B §10: scoring and meaning | Ask whether awarded points reflect the intended ability | Does not prescribe a universal metric |
| Q11 | Part A §3; Part B §10.2 internal structure | Combine evidence for score aggregation and separate score interpretations | Omits dedicated factor-analytic and item-model criteria |
| Q12 | Part B §9: reliability/precision | Extend marking checks to software and AI graders | One question compresses several kinds of scorer error |
| Q13 | Part B §8 norms and reference interpretation | Replace assumed human norms with justified human, chance, or model comparisons | Does not imply that arbitrary model cohorts are equivalent to population norms |
| Q14 | Part B §9.3 temporal stability | Adapt repeated administration to repeated runs of a fixed AI setup | A study's existence does not imply favorable results |
| Q15 | Part B §9.4 equivalence and §11 fair use | Adapt concerns about equivalent forms and administration to changed instructions/presentation/setup | Prompt variation is not automatically a parallel-form study |
| Q16 | Part B §9 reliability/precision | Simplify estimates of variation relative to intended comparisons | Score uncertainty does not replace internal-consistency evidence |
| Q17 | Part B §10 validity | Extend alternative explanations to shortcuts for earning points | Does not enumerate all possible shortcuts |
| Q18 | Part B §10 validity | Add AI-specific prior training exposure and its controls | No simple human-test equivalent; safeguards do not establish a particular model's history |
| Q19 | Part B §§10–11 validity and fair use | Add benchmark-specific training and repeated-submission rules | Acceptable adaptation depends on intended use, not a blanket ban |
| Q20 | Part B §10: evidence supporting interpretation beyond observed tasks | Retain the need for evidence when extending claims | No generalization is implied by a broad title |
| Q21 | Part B §11 fair use | Replace human groups alone with relevant AI systems and evaluation situations | Human fairness and system comparability are related but not identical |
| Q22 | Part A §5 and Part B §7 materials | Adapt availability and inspectability to data, instructions and software | Controlled access need not mean all review is impossible |
| Q23 | Part A §5 and Part B §7 materials | Combine access/reuse rules with responsible-use protections | Different legal, ethical and technical issues remain compressed |
| Q24 | Documentation/materials functions in §§5–7 | Add explicit tracking of changing benchmark releases and score comparisons | No exact one-to-one EFPA version-control item is claimed |
| Q25 | Part B §§10 and 12 validity and reports | Adapt personal reports to published benchmark claims | No detailed review of every report's presentation feature |

## Omitted and transformed functions

The questionnaire does not retain EFPA's detailed human test-user qualification categories, population norm tables, or separate technical assessments of internal consistency and item-response models. These omissions make the questionnaire shorter but can remove important checks. They are not proof that those topics have no AI counterpart. MESA does not establish measurement equivalence with EFPA and does not convert EFPA ratings to its five categories.

The transformation of human test subjects into AI systems changes administration, exposure, reference populations and score interpretation. Software settings, training exposure and release changes need explicit attention. The full source is used in the comparison condition to expose, not conceal, difficulties with this transformation.
