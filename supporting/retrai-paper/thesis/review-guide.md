# Short guide to using MESA

1. **Name the decision.** Write the benchmark version, the score, and the claim you are checking. “Can these scores support a small difference between two systems?” is more precise than “Is this a good benchmark?”
2. **Record sources once.** Give each source an ID. In Part A, record facts and missing information. Later answers can refer to the same source and Part A item.
3. **Answer only questions that apply.** Use `n/a` with a reason. A narrow claim does not require proof of general intelligence. The scorecard is a starting point; consult omitted full-template items when the benchmark needs them, especially scorer checks.
4. **Rate with a reason.** Use a short note: source, finding, missing information, rating reason, and limit on the conclusion. A source reference alone is not a reason. Do not average ratings.
5. **Resolve disagreements explicitly.** Check whether reviewers used the same sources and judged the same use. Record the original judgments and the reason for the final choice.
6. **Finish with the decision.** State what the scores support, what remains unsupported, and what conditions must stay fixed for comparison.

Example: “Run settings are published, but repeat-run results were not found. Stability cannot be judged from settings alone. Small ranking differences remain unresolved.”

## Record effort during human trials

For each session, record the participant's experience, template version, start/end time, and time spent finding sources, making judgments, and writing. Record confusing questions, repeated work, omissions, and AI help. Leave unmeasured times blank and label them unmeasured.

This guide is shorter than the template because detailed definitions remain there. No time saving has yet been measured.
