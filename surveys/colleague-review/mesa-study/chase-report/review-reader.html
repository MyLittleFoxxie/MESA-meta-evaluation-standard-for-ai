About these reviews

What are they?

Claude Sonnet 5.5 read written descriptions of two AI benchmarks and produced these reviews. It did not run the benchmarks. ARC-AGI-2 tests solving grid puzzles. DesignQA asks questions about engineering documents.

Why did we ask for them?

The study examines how different review instructions affect what the model discusses, misses, or interprets. This helps us identify difficulties in adapting methods for reviewing human tests to AI benchmarks.

What were the three approaches?

  • MESA: 25 questions, answer choices, explanations, and a completed teaching example.
  • EFPA-TRM: the complete 2025 model for reviewing human tests, supplied as text from all 90 pages.
  • General instructions: a request to examine the benchmark’s construction, scoring, comparisons, consistency, interpretation, fairness, and reporting, without either questionnaire.

All three approaches received the same benchmark descriptions. Only MESA received a completed example, so the comparison concerns the whole set of supplied materials.

Why are there only six reviews on this page?

The benchmark task used five separate conversations for each approach. Each conversation reviewed ARC-AGI-2 and then DesignQA: 15 conversations produced 30 benchmark reviews. This page shows one conversation for each approach, so we can examine its two reviews in detail.

Three approaches × two benchmarks = six selected reviews. The other responses remain in the study records. These six are examples for close reading, not the entire experiment.

Why were these particular six selected?

They come from the conversation labelled repetition 1 for each approach. The run numbers were assigned before each batch ran; they do not tell us which conversation was launched first. These reviews were not chosen because they were the best responses. A selected response would not have been replaced just because its format was invalid.

How do they fit into the wider study?

Another 15 conversations examined short invented cases, asking what each description supported, contradicted, or left unknown. Across both tasks, the reporting selection contains 30 conversations, with 28 usable responses. Two completed responses did not meet the required format and were excluded from analysis, but kept in the records. One was a benchmark conversation containing two reviews, so 28 of the 30 benchmark reviews were usable.

The overall reporting selection was assembled after the runs. MESA guidance was finalized after development testing, and its conversations ran after the comparison batches. This history is separate from using repetition 1 for the six reviews shown here. The methods were not run together in a randomly assigned experiment.

What can these six reviews tell us?

They illustrate differences and interpretation problems. They do not establish how common those problems are, which method is best, or how people would respond. These are repeated responses from one model, not reviews by six different people.

For the full account, see the study guide, record of selected conversations, and analysis of these six reviews.

What you should check

These are model responses, not verified facts. A statement listed as a problem may still need correction. Compare the response with the supplied reading and our analysis. Check whether we quoted it correctly, described it fairly, missed anything, or drew a conclusion the reading does not support.

Selected repetition 1 · MESA

MESA — ARC-AGI-2

Original response file (both benchmarks) · Choose another review

Overall review

ARC-AGI-2 is clearly specified: the version, the problem sets, the scoring software and the task format are all described. The creators give reasons for the design and used a human study to match difficulty across the test sets. Key gaps remain. There is no estimate of run-to-run variation, no systematic test of changed setups, and no study linking scores to abilities beyond grid puzzles. Exposure and shortcut protections are partial, and the change record does not recalculate affected scores. Claims about broader reasoning go beyond the grid-problem evidence.

Claims the model says are supported

  • The test covers ARC-AGI-2 public problems, official scoring software and ARC Prize verified-test rules, and the version, problem sets and scoring are identified.
  • Credit requires an exact match in size and every square, with two attempts per input, and problem scores are averaged.
  • Matching the three test sets for human difficulty was based on a study with 407 people in 515 sessions.
  • The materials show no evidence that the scores measure anything beyond two-dimensional grid problems.

Problems listed by the model

  • Public problems are visible, so prior exposure remains possible, and ARC Prize acknowledges hidden-test information could still escape.
  • Corrections to coloured squares and unclear examples were needed after release.
  • The change record does not recalculate every affected AI score.
  • Verified testing uses one run, and other testing routes may use different conditions.
  • The creators' links to broader intelligence are not supported by the supplied studies.

Information the model says is missing

  • Estimates or ranges showing how much the same AI's score changes across repeated runs.
  • Systematic tests of changed instructions, grid presentation or software.
  • A comparison point such as a human or guessing score matched to the AI testing conditions.
  • Evidence that scores predict performance in language, social interaction or real-world work.
  • Independent testing of the whole marking system.
  • How the number of 120 problems per test set was justified.
  • The complete archive of public outputs, which the preparer did not check.

How the model interprets terms for AI

  • Within this test, 'reasoning' means inferring a rule from grid examples and producing an exact grid, not general intelligence.
  • A 'problem score' is the average of its inputs, so 0.5 is partial credit, not 'solved'.
  • 'Training allowed on development problems' means exposure to development tasks is permitted, while tuning specifically for the evaluation tasks is discouraged.
  • 'Verified' means run under ARC Prize's stated conditions, not shown to be stable or repeatable.

Model’s closing note

Some questions overlap, such as 14 versus 16 and 17 versus 18. It was also unclear whether the human study counts as a comparison point for AI scores.

Selected repetition 1 · MESA

MESA — DesignQA

Original response file (both benchmarks) · Choose another review

Overall review

DesignQA is clearly described: its version, question groups, inputs and scoring software are identified, and the tasks resemble parts of engineering work. Its evidence is limited. It uses one rulebook, the groups are very uneven, and wording and formatting affect marking. Systems receive different information, there is no human baseline, and score variation is poorly characterized. Some robustness checks exist, but there is no full repeat-run study. Materials are only partly reproducible, and licensing, change records and reuse rules are unclear. The paper itself limits claims about other documents.

Claims the model says are supported

  • The test is the original 2024 DesignQA with 1,451 question-answer pairs in six groups, plus public scoring software.
  • Groups are very uneven, from 1,192 questions in one group to 16 in another.
  • The overall score weights the six groups equally.
  • Wording and formatting can affect credit.
  • Systems differ in the information they receive, so results describe the AI plus its information supply.
  • The evidence does not support claims about other technical documents or real engineering projects.

Problems listed by the model

  • Marking examples include missing commas and valid related rules absent from the supplied answer.
  • One released answer file has fewer rows than the paper's question count.
  • The exact historical software, settings and inputs are not fully linked to each result.
  • Always answering 'yes' would earn credit on the described scale-bar drawings.
  • The rulebook and questions are public, and no protected set or overlap check was established.
  • Reviewer disagreement rates and remaining errors are not reported.

Information the model says is missing

  • Reviewer disagreement and remaining error counts.
  • A complete marking accuracy and consistency study.
  • A human engineer baseline.
  • Identical-condition repeated runs and ranges for the main comparisons.
  • Rules on training on the questions and on repeated submission.
  • A licence file and complete responsible-reuse rules.
  • A clear change record explaining the effects of revisions on scores.
  • Direct checks of exposure for each AI's training data.

How the model interprets terms for AI

  • 'Understanding engineering documents' here means performance on one Formula SAE rulebook and a few task types.
  • An AI's result reflects the AI together with its retrieved or supplied passages.
  • The overall score is an equal-weight average of six unequal groups and not shown to measure one skill.
  • Marking by text similarity measures wording match as well as correctness.

Model’s closing note

Some questions overlap, such as 14 and 15, and the guidance for one run with varied passages was ambiguous. Question 23 could be read as either missing rules or evidence against reuse.

SCOPE AND METHOD.

Missing evidence is rated 0 (not possible to rate), which is not evidence of poor quality. Ratings are provisional section-level judgements. No aggregate score is computed. Critical attributes: scoring, repeat-run precision, contamination control, validity for the stated reasoning claims. Where the intended use is comparing AI systems, a 0 on a critical attribute means any high-stakes use should be restricted to research or expert use.

Scores support statements about performance on these tasks under stated conditions, not engineering competence broadly or fine rankings.

Claims the model says are supported

  • The benchmark draws on real Formula SAE materials and has 1,451 questions in six groups.
  • It is limited to one rulebook and a small range of task types, as the authors acknowledge.
  • Marking is sensitive to wording and formatting.
  • The overall score weights six unequal groups equally.
  • Systems receive different information, so results reflect the AI plus its information supply.
  • One extra correct answer in the 16-question group moves accuracy 6.25 points.
  • Broader claims about engineering competence or fine distinctions between close systems are not supported.

Problems listed by the model

  • Groups are very uneven in size (1,192 vs 16).
  • No evidence that overall or group scores measure distinct skills.
  • Wording and formatting affect credit; valid related rules may be missing from the reference answers.
  • Explanations received only a limited check.
  • Reviewer disagreement and remaining error counts are not reported.
  • Marking accuracy and consistency have not been fully studied.
  • Not every system receives the same information.
  • No human engineer baseline.
  • Variation was examined only through changed passages, not identical repeats.
  • All scale-bar drawings comply, so a constant 'yes' would earn credit on that portion.
  • Possible exposure to similar images is discussed but not directly checked.
  • No protected test set or systematic overlap check, and unclear training and resubmission rules.
  • Historical software, settings and inputs are not fully linked to each result.
  • One released answer file has fewer rows than the paper's question count.
  • No clear change record, licence file or reuse rules found.

Information the model says is missing

  • Reviewer disagreement rates and remaining error rates.
  • Human engineer performance.
  • Repeat-run variation for identical tests.
  • Marking validity and consistency study.
  • Evidence of distinct skills for the group scores.
  • Test for always-yes or other non-understanding strategies.
  • Training-data overlap and contamination checks.
  • Rules on training and repeated submissions.
  • Complete records to reproduce every paper result.
  • Explanation of the discrepancy in the answer file's row count.
  • Effects of code revisions on scores.
  • Licence and reuse terms.
  • Performance on other technical documents.

How the model interprets terms for AI

  • 'Understanding engineering documents' here means performance on questions from one Formula SAE rulebook and its drawings under particular marking rules and information supply.
  • An AI's score reflects the model combined with how rules are supplied (full text vs retrieval).
  • The overall score is an equal-weight combination of groups, not a measure of overall engineering competence.

Model’s closing note

The instructions were clear. The phrase 'changes of meaning for AI' was interpreted as how terms such as understanding or reasoning change meaning when applied to AI. The instruction on a closing note was followed, and no aggregate score was calculated.
