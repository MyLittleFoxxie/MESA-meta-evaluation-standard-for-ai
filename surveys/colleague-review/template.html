What you are being asked to do

An AI benchmark is a test used to assess an AI system. You will review how two benchmarks are designed, scored and used. You are evaluating the tests, not answering their test problems or rating particular AI models.

Complete the same 25 questions for each benchmark: 50 selections in total, grouped into six topics. The first five concern the benchmark’s description; the remaining twenty concern its methods and claims. There is no time limit, no answer key and no total quality score.

Read the passage about the first benchmark, then answer its 25 questions by interpreting what you have read. Repeat this for the second benchmark. You may return to either passage at any time; this is not a memory test. Judge the described version and intended use using only this packet. No source reading is required. The summaries include documented facts and clearly identified limits; they cannot settle every issue. “Not sure” is a useful response when the information is insufficient.

Comments are optional. You may use them to record a qualification or confusing wording. You do not need to cite sources or justify each answer.

How to choose an answer

YesThe supplied information supports the whole statement or condition.
PartlyIt supports some parts, but not all.
NoThe information shows that the condition is not met.
Not sureInformation is missing, unclear or insufficient to decide.
Not applicableThe question does not apply to this benchmark or intended use.

A missing study is not proof that a test fails. For “Was it tested?”, consider whether testing happened; a Yes does not mean the results were good. For “Is there evidence supporting…?”, consider whether the findings support the claim. An unanswered item stays blank; it is not recorded as No.

A few useful terms

Under each numbered question, “Meaning” explains the wording. It is not an extra question. Choose just one answer for the numbered question.
