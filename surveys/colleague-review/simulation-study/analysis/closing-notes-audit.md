# Exploratory audit of generated MESA closing notes

Status: complete. All 40 benchmark closing notes from the 20 planned Sol MESA real-benchmark sessions were inspected. Nineteen selected responses came from attempt 1; `real-mesa-research_assistant-3` came from the permitted nested `attempt-2/response.txt` after its original process failure. Each session contributes two notes once. Controlled probes and other models are excluded. All 40 notes are nonempty. Each note is generated text, not a participant or an independent human observation.

The post-observation plan is `analyst/closing-note-guide.md`. Sources are the `closing_note` fields in `series/gpt-6.1-sol/outputs/real-mesa-*/response.txt`, inspected against the frozen `materials/mesa.txt` and the original `materials/real-passages.txt`. The same assistant helped design, execute and interpret this study. The observations are exploratory and have no independent adjudication.

## What the comments identify

Several notes distinguish whether checks occurred from how extensive they were. For example, `real-mesa-neutral-1`, ARC, says: “Items 8 and 12 can blur whether checking occurred with whether it was comprehensive.” The frozen questionnaire already says, “For whether something was tested, Yes means testing occurred, not that results were favourable.” The unresolved issue in these comments is therefore the required scope or completeness of checking, rather than an absent instruction about favourable outcomes. The DesignQA note from `real-mesa-newcomer-3` makes its own choice explicit: “I used Partly for limited marking checks and Yes where changes to the testing setup were explicitly tested.” This is a reported interpretation, not evidence of the model's hidden reason for each answer.

Scope also changes the judgment. The ARC note from `real-mesa-benchmark_reviewer-4` says: “Item 22 depends on whether its scope is the public evaluation or all verified hidden evaluations.” The supplied passage identifies public problems, scoring software and verified-testing policies, while distinguishing controlled hidden data and other evaluation routes. The comment identifies a boundary that should be carried into the explanation of a category. For DesignQA, `real-mesa-research_assistant-2` says: “Item 21 should clarify whether fairness concerns complete AI-plus-retrieval systems or the AI models alone.” The passage explicitly treats performance as that of the AI together with its information supply. The note therefore motivates making that unit explicit when comparing systems, rather than establishing that the supplied passage omitted it.

The DesignQA note from `real-mesa-neutral-2` identifies a combined question: “Item 11 combines aggregation support with distinct-skill interpretation.” The original item explicitly asks about both. The comment highlights a possible tension when evidence supports the aggregation procedure but does not establish separate ability measures; it is not evidence that either part should be dropped. The ARC note from the same run also distinguishes human difficulty studies from matched human performance baselines.

The DesignQA note from `real-mesa-newcomer-4` identifies an applicability boundary: “Item 20 could be read as not applicable when broader transfer is explicitly left uncertain; Not sure reflects the stated interest in those broader claims.” That distinction depends on whether the intended use includes a claim beyond the supplied tasks; acknowledging uncertainty is not itself evidence that such a claim is absent. The selected retry, `real-mesa-research_assistant-3`, separately says that combined uncertainty, fairness and documentation conditions can make Partly versus Not sure unclear. These are generated requests for interpretation guidance, not independently observed category errors.

The notes retain some distinctions already explained in the passage and questionnaire. `real-mesa-neutral-1`, DesignQA, says: “Item 16 should distinguish uncertainty under changed passages from uncertainty under identical repeated conditions.” `real-mesa-research_assistant-1`, ARC, asks for clarity about “fractional problem credit or fully solved problems.” These comments can indicate where a reviewer wants the distinction kept visible; they do not by themselves show that the distinction was missing from the reading material. Notes about unavailable reuse rules similarly should not be read as evidence that reuse is prohibited.

The ARC and DesignQA notes from `real-mesa-benchmark_reviewer-3` and `real-mesa-newcomer-1`, and the DesignQA note from `real-mesa-research_assistant-5` explicitly question the experiment's phrase “condition-native review” (five of the 40 notes). This phrase comes from the experimental output instructions/schema, not the canonical MESA questionnaire. In `real-mesa-benchmark_reviewer-3` it was interpreted as “a review of questionnaire fit and clarity for this benchmark”; in `real-mesa-research_assistant-5` as “a passage-grounded review from the requested research-assistant perspective.” These are materially different readings of the requested free-text field. The executed instructions must remain frozen; a future protocol should instead request “a review using the assigned method.” This experimental ambiguity limits comparison of native review prose and must not be reported as a problem with MESA's participant-facing wording.

## Limits

A closing note is prompted output. It can restate the material, request extra clarity, or discuss the experimental instructions. It does not establish that the questionnaire caused an answer, that the commenter misunderstood it, or that people would experience the same issue. The observations concern this model, supplied passages and experiment. Empty or absent comments would not establish absence of difficulty. No human comprehension, comparative review accuracy, or general ambiguity prevalence is inferred.
