## Findings limited to these reviews

All four methods retain narrow task interpretations, scoring distinctions, missing repeated-evaluation evidence and several documentation gaps. This does not establish complete EFPA-TRM application or superior coverage.

Original MESA explicitly lists missing DesignQA reviewer-disagreement rates. Revised MESA separates checking provided answers from testing marking, but does not explicitly ask for those reviewers’ agreement rate. General instructions and EFPA-TRM identify that gap. This is a difference between selected responses, not a questionnaire omission or frequency estimate.

Original MESA selects Partly for DesignQA question 12. Revised MESA selects Yes and explains that some marking tests occurred but coverage was limited. These are recorded choices, not adjudicated correct answers. Neither establishes complete accuracy.

For ARC question 14, revised MESA selects No on the basis of the verified-testing one-run policy, while original MESA selects Not sure. This illustrates a remaining scope decision: an official procedure does not necessarily establish the absence of other repeated-run research. No correctness key for real reviews is supplied.

The original MESA, revised MESA and general-instruction ARC reviews all place a single evaluation under documented problems. That reported procedure alone does not demonstrate harmful score variation. Revised guidance does not remove this interpretation issue in the selected response.

The EFPA-TRM ARC review lists imperfect hidden-task confidentiality as a problem, citing the possibility that information could escape. This describes risk rather than an observed leak. DesignQA section 6.2.11 partly bases an inadequacy rating on responsible-reuse and exposure-management instructions not being established; that missing information alone does not demonstrate inadequate underlying rules. Other EFPA judgments explicitly distinguish missing information from demonstrated problems. These qualifications concern this response, not all EFPA-TRM reviews.

General instructions and EFPA-TRM retain the 6.25 percentage-point consequence of one answer in the 16-question group. Both MESA reviews discuss uncertainty without that number. EFPA-TRM explicitly separates human demographic findings from fair AI comparisons; the other selected reviews are less explicit. A detail absent in one response is not necessarily omitted by the questionnaire.

These observations provide no consistent improvement attributable to revised help. The reading is illustrative, assistant-interpreted and includes labeled format-invalid responses. It does not estimate error rates or human benefits.
